The calendar is only as current as the inbox.

Most scheduling still happens over email. Keeping a principal’s calendar in step with it is careful, manual work.

  1. Requests hide in threads

    A time proposed three replies deep. A forward that says “can you set this up?” A CC you only joined today.

  2. Each one becomes a hold by hand

    Read it, check the calendar, create the event, and hope the context behind it survives.

  3. New meetings land on old ones

    When two commitments collide, someone has to notice, decide what moves, and move it.

From scheduling email to calendar, in four steps.

  1. 01

    It reads the mail

    ZipSched reads scheduling email and picks out who is asking, when, and for how long.

  2. 02

    It checks the calendar

    The request is checked against your principal’s calendar, including any meeting it would collide with.

  3. 03

    It suggests the change

    A new hold, a move, or a draft reply with times, with the source email attached.

  4. 04

    You approve it

    Review the suggestion beside the email it came from, approve it, and the calendar updates.

Every suggestion arrives with its source.

The email it came from
Each suggestion is attached to the message that prompted it, so you can check the ask before you act on it.
Collisions come with a proposed move
When a new meeting lands on an existing one, the suggestion names the overlap and proposes where the other meeting could go.
Find-times replies wait as drafts
When someone asks for times, the reply is written as an unsent draft in your principal’s mailbox, waiting for your review.

Built around the tools you already use.

  • Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace

    ZipSched works with Microsoft 365 / Outlook and with Google Workspace, whichever your principal’s organization runs.

  • Text it to schedule

    Send a text such as “find 30 minutes with Sam next week,” and the answer comes back by text.

  • Replies wait as drafts

    Answers to find-times requests are written as unsent drafts in the principal’s mailbox, ready for review.

  • Tomorrow, at 5 pm

    An evening email lays out the next day’s calendar, so tomorrow is settled the night before.

What it isn’t

  • A replacement for you. It does the reading and the cross-checking. Knowing your principal is still your job.
  • Every calendar system. Microsoft 365 / Outlook and Google Workspace only.
